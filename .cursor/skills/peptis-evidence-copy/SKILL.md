# Peptis Evidence Copy

## Source first

Read `../../../brand-kit/Peptis_GLP1_Body_Recomposition_Evidence_Dossier_Final.docx` before drafting
or approving claims. Extract `word/document.xml` with Python `zipfile` and XML tools if the DOCX
cannot be read directly. Use original papers, official labels and public datasets cited by the
dossier. Do not cite another brand's summary.

Read [reference.md](reference.md) for the short approved statistics list. Check the dossier when
the needed claim is not listed there.

## Six core conclusions

1. Weight loss and fat loss are not the same. A better outcome keeps strength, function and a
   higher share of lean tissue while excess fat falls.
2. Lean mass can decline during substantial weight loss, including GLP-1 assisted weight loss.
   Fat generally falls more, and measured lean mass is not all skeletal muscle.
3. Lower appetite can make protein, fluids, fiber and micronutrients harder to obtain.
4. Resistance training has the strongest behavioral evidence for retaining lean tissue and
   strength during weight loss. Protein supports training but does not replace it.
5. Loose skin mainly reflects tissue volume loss, pace of loss and baseline skin quality.
   Exercise can support underlying contour but cannot remove substantial excess skin.
6. Maintenance begins early. Withdrawal studies show that regain is common when treatment stops,
   which reflects chronic biology rather than personal failure.

## Claim guardrails

- Never equate lean mass with skeletal muscle. DEXA lean mass includes water, organs, connective
  tissue, bone mineral and skeletal muscle.
- Use approximately 25% when discussing the estimated lean portion of total weight lost. Never
  say that up to 40% of lost weight is muscle.
- Describe resistance training as the strongest supported behavioral intervention. State when
  evidence comes from general weight loss rather than GLP-1 specific research.
- Present protein as supportive and individualized. Do not use one body weight denominator for
  everyone or imply that protein replaces resistance training.
- Never claim that a supplement prevents, treats or reverses GLP-1 medication side effects.
- Keep current capability distinct from future service. A reservation is not care, prescribing,
  medication, clinician review or pharmacy fulfillment. Future service depends on launch, state
  access, eligibility, final terms and affirmative enrollment.
- Put population, study design and important limitations near every statistic.
- Do not borrow medication trial outcomes as Peptis outcomes.
- Do not invent testimonials, results, crossed out prices or fake discounts.

## Copy style

- Write calm, direct sentences with concrete benefits first and concise caveats nearby.
- Use natural language that sounds edited by an experienced person.
- Do not use decorative em dashes.
- Keep necessary terms such as `GLP-1` and `state-by-state`.
- Avoid slogan fragments, staccato lists, fear, shame, competitor attacks and generic AI patterns.
- Avoid "not just X, but Y," "journey," "unlock," "revolutionary," "architecture," "matrix,"
  "optimize" and repeated "planned."
- Do not overwhelm every section with legal language. Keep complete limitations in the FAQ or
  footer, with short pricing and capability caveats where decisions are made.

## Infographic checklist

- Use only dossier supported figures and show the source nearby.
- Label whether a visual presents trial evidence, general weight loss evidence or a Peptis
  program framework.
- Give charts semantic figure markup, an accessible title and a plain language explanation.
- Explain denominators and measurement limits.
- State that study averages do not guarantee individual outcomes.
- Include the evidence review date and an education only notice.
- Preserve sufficient contrast and reduced motion behavior.
- Confirm that the visual does not imply Peptis produced the cited outcome.
- Current homepage visuals: 75/25 composition, five part framework, measures beyond the scale, protein target, evidence cards and founding offer comparison.
